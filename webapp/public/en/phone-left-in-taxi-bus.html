Phone left in a taxi, a ride-share or a bus: what to do?

Updated November 16, 2026

A phone left in a vehicle isn't necessarily stolen: the driver or a passenger often finds it. It all happens in the first hour, while the vehicle isn't far and the battery lasts.

In the first hour

  1. Call it right away

    With a friend's phone, call your number and send a text with another number where you can be reached. The driver or a passenger may answer.

  2. Taxi or ride-share booked in an app

    Open the app from another device: most have a "lost item" section to contact the driver. Keep the trip receipt.

  3. Bus, tram or train: note the line and time

    Contact the company's customer service or lost and found with the line, direction, time and a description of the phone.

  4. Ring it and locate it

    In Find Hub (google.com/android/find), see where it is and make it ring. A vehicle moving on the map confirms it's still on board.

If no one answers

  1. Show a message on the screen

    With "Mark as lost", the phone locks and shows your message and a number to call.

  2. Follow its location without chasing it

    Note the stops or the depot where the vehicle ends up and pass them on to the company. Don't chase a vehicle yourself.

  3. Check lost and found again

    Items are often handed in at the end of the shift. Call again the next day and the days after.

  4. No news after 24 to 48 hours

    Block the SIM card, change your passwords and follow our guide "Phone stolen: what to do?".

So you don't lose it on the road again

  • Get into the habit of checking the seat and the door before you get out.
  • Write a "lost phone" message in HearMe with a number where you can be reached.
  • Check that Find Hub (Find My Device) is on.
  • Note your IMEI (*#06#) and keep it outside the phone.

Frequently asked questions

Can the driver give my phone back?

Yes, very often. Contact them through the booking app or through the taxi or transport company, giving the time and the route.

How do I know if it's still in the vehicle?

Check its location in Find Hub or on gethearme.me: if it moves like a vehicle, it's probably still on board.

What if the battery is dead?

You'll often see its last known location. Give it to lost and found and read our guide "Phone switched off or battery dead".
