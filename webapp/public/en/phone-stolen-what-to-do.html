Phone stolen: what to do in the first 10 minutes

Updated November 16, 2026

Having your phone stolen is stressful, and it's easy to lose time. Here's what to do, in order, to keep a chance of getting it back and, above all, protect your accounts, your money and your photos.

In the first 10 minutes

  1. Don't chase the thief

    Your safety comes before the phone. Move away, get somewhere safe and use a relative's phone or a computer.

  2. Locate the phone

    With a Google account, open google.com/android/find. If HearMe is installed, sign in at gethearme.me: live location, ring, lock and a photo of the person. Don't go and get it alone: give the location to the police.

  3. Lock it remotely

    From Find My Device or gethearme.me, lock the screen and show a message with a number where you can be reached.

  4. Block your SIM card

    Call your carrier (from another phone or their website) to suspend the line. The thief can no longer make calls or receive your SMS codes.

Within the next hour

  1. Change your important passwords

    Your Google account first, then your e-mail, social networks and payment apps. Sign the stolen phone out of those accounts if they offer it.

  2. Tell your bank

    If banking apps or a saved card were on the phone, call your bank to watch or block payments.

  3. Report the theft to the police

    Bring the phone's IMEI number. Your carrier can then block the phone itself with that number, even with another SIM card.

  4. Tell your insurer

    If the phone is insured, a police report is almost always required. Keep a copy.

Where to find the IMEI number

On the phone's box, on the receipt, or in your Google account (myaccount.google.com, Security, Your devices). For next time: dial *#06# on your phone and write the number down somewhere safe.

For next time: get ready in 5 minutes

  • Write down your IMEI number (*#06#) and keep the box or receipt.
  • Use a PIN or fingerprint lock, never an unprotected screen.
  • Check that Find My Device is turned on in your Google settings.
  • Install HearMe: an alarm if someone grabs the phone, a photo of the person on every wrong code, live location on gethearme.me and alerts to your relatives on Telegram.

Frequently asked questions

Can you find a phone that's switched off?

Not live: a phone that's off no longer sends its location. You usually see the last known location before it went off. HearMe also warns you when the battery is almost empty or the phone is switched off.

Should I erase the phone remotely?

Only as a last resort, if you think you'll never get it back: once erased, you can no longer locate it.

Can the thief use my phone with another SIM card?

After your report, the carrier can block the phone with its IMEI. That's why it matters to know this number.
